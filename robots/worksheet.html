Part A: WH-Question Detective Clues Match the question word to what it seeks
1. WHAT? asks for a thing or action.
e.g. "What does it look like?"
2. WHERE? asks for a place or environment.
e.g. "Where does it swim or go?"
3. WHEN? asks for a time or year.
e.g. "When was it made?"
4. WHO? asks for a person or creator.
e.g. "Who invented or built it?"
5. WHY? asks for a reason or problem.
e.g. "Why did scientists build it?"
6. HOW? asks for the method or operation.
e.g. "How does it move?"

🧑‍🏫 TEACHER GUIDE & ANSWER KEY

Page 4: 60–70 Minute Lesson Plan & Factual Verification

Level: A1–A2 (Ages 9–12)
Format: Team-based (3–4 students)
Lesson Roadmap (60–70 Minutes) 9 Structured Phases
Phase Timing Teacher Actions & Smartboard Student Team Tasks
1. Welcome 5 min Introduce 5 missions. Assign groups of 3–4. Emphasize teamwork, not competition. Join group, receive worksheet, pick roles (Reader, Writer, Spokesperson).
2. Mystery 8–10 min Show Stage 2 visual. Do NOT reveal robot name! Elicit WH predictions. Fill in Worksheet Page 1 with 6 WH guesses. Sketch the robot.
3. Detective 6–8 min Stage 3 interactive matching: What, Where, When, Who, Why, How. Master WH question meanings; practice pronunciation aloud.
4. Dossiers 10 min Display official reading text (100–140 words). Play audio narration. Read group dossier, underline answers to the 6 WH questions.
5. Answers 8 min Guide comprehension checks on Stage 5. Use "Peek at Reading" drawer. Answer Worksheet Page 2 questions; check scores together.
6. Fact/Guess 6–8 min Display Stage 6 statements. Prompt humorous vs real science debate. Vote REAL or GUESS; learn surprising scientific facts.
7. Prep 10 min Model presentation frame. Ensure every student has a speaking turn. Complete Page 3 speech bubble; rehearse team presentation.
8. Reveal 10 min Invite groups to present (1–2 min each). Reveal authentic science and jobs. Present to class; listen to peers and note uses on Page 3.
9. Reflection 5 min Facilitate whole-class vote: Most useful? Most surprising? Award badges. Vote on favorites; complete exit reflection; celebrate learning!

🐟 Group 1: Fish Robot (NYU Biomimetic Swimmer)

• What: A robotic fish with jointed tail • Where: Rivers and water channels • When: 2010s • Who: Prof. Maurizio Porfiri (NYU) • Why: Guide fish away from dangerous turbine dams and toxic spills • How: Swims with rhythmic tail movements that live fish recognize as a leader • Fact/Guess: Leader fish = REAL; underwater pizza = GUESS.

🪼 Group 2: Robotic Jellyfish (FAU / Max Planck Soft Explorer)

• What: A soft translucent robot with flexible silicone tentacles • Where: Coral reefs and coastal waters • When: 2018–2020s • Who: Florida Atlantic University & Max Planck Institute • Why: Study fragile marine life without chopping them with propeller blades • How: Water hydraulic pulses through rubber channels • Fact/Guess: No blades = REAL; laser party lights = GUESS.

🐝 Group 3: Robotic Bee (Harvard RoboBee)

• What: A micro-robot the size of a paperclip • Where: Small crevices, disaster sites, flowers • When: 2013 • Who: Harvard Microrobotics Lab • Why: Search collapsed spaces and study tiny aerodynamics • How: Piezoelectric artificial muscles flapping 120 times/sec • Fact/Guess: Flaps 120 times/sec = REAL; makes strawberry honey = GUESS.

🐍 Group 4: Snake Robot (Carnegie Mellon Search & Rescue)

• What: A modular multi-jointed robot • Where: Earthquake ruins and industrial pipes • When: 2000s–present • Who: CMU Biorobotics Lab • Why: Locate trapped disaster survivors where humans/dogs cannot fit • How: Sinusoidal wave slithering with camera/light in its head • Fact/Guess: Climbs trees & pipes = REAL; dances to flute music = GUESS.

🐕 Group 5: Robot Dog (Boston Dynamics / Spot)

• What: A four-legged agile mobile robot • Where: Power stations, mines, construction sites • When: 2015–present • Who: Boston Dynamics engineers • Why: Inspect hazardous locations and radioactive/gas zones • How: 4 articulating legs with lidar sensors and dynamic self-balancing • Fact/Guess: Climbs stairs & balances = REAL; sleeps in doghouse = GUESS.
