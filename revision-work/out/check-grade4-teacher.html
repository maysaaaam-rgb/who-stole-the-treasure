Teacher Guide: English Progress Check · Grade 4 · Term 1

Pupil paper: check-grade4.html · Content: Global Readings 3 Literacy Review, Unit 1 "I Love Reading", Alice Tea Party Home Practice patterns (set for 4A and 4B). No Unit 2 content. Restaurant language is not included because the records do not list it.

Paper: 38 marks, about 38 minutes (A 8 · B 8 · C 6 · D 5 · E 6 · F 5). Speaking: 12 marks, 1–2 minutes per pupil, done one to one while the class does a quiet task. Total 50.

1. Listening script (read each part TWICE)

Say: "Section A. Listen. Circle the picture. Number 1."

1. "The caterpillar is on the branch." (pause 5 seconds, read again)

2. "Alice chased a White Rabbit."

3. "The monarch butterfly goes on a long journey."

4. "Reading is a workout for your brain."

Say: "Now A2. Listen to the story about Alice. Then circle T or F."

"Alice was sitting by the river with her sister. Her sister was reading a book. The book had no pictures. Alice was bored and sleepy. Suddenly, a White Rabbit ran past. 'Oh dear! I am late!' he said. Alice was very surprised. She chased the rabbit. She fell down a very deep hole. On the walls of the hole, she saw shelves with books and jars."

Give pupils 30 seconds to read the four A2 sentences before you start. Read the story twice at a slow, natural speed.

3. Marking rules (partial credit)

  • A1, A2, C1, D1, D2, E1: 1 mark each. If two answers are circled, give 0.
  • B1, B2: 1 mark each. Accept small copying errors if the word is clearly right (e.g. caterpiller). For 🦅 the answer must be predator (from the box); do not accept bird or eagle. Capital letters are not needed.
  • C2: 2 = all words in the correct order. 1 = one word out of place or missing. 0 = more errors.
  • E2.5: accept any answer that shows the topic: the brain and/or reading. E2.6: accept message or small spelling errors.
  • F1: 2 = complete sentence about Alice with a correct past verb (chased, fell, saw, was). 1 = meaning is clear but the verb is wrong (Alice fall down) or it is not a full sentence. 0 = blank or not about the story.
  • F2: 2 = complete sentence with I am / I have / I like and a sensible ending. 1 = meaning clear with one grammar error (I am like cake). 0 = blank or unrelated.
  • F3: 1 = both sentences start with a capital letter and end with a full stop.
  • Never take marks off for messy handwriting if you can read it. Accept British or American spelling.

5. Speaking task and rubric (one page)

Set-up: one pupil at a time, 1–2 minutes. Use one Alice Tea Party character card (from the Home Practice + Cards set). Be friendly and give one repeat if needed.

PromptTeacher saysExample good answer
1 Alice questions"Who did Alice chase? Where did she fall? How did she feel at the start?""She chased the White Rabbit. She fell down a hole. She was bored and sleepy."
2 Character card"You are this character. Tell me about you." Then ask: "Do you like…? Can you…?""I am the Mad Hatter. I have a big hat. I like tea." "Yes, I do." "No, I can't."
3 Brain fact"Tell me one thing about your brain and reading.""Reading is a workout for my brain. My brain cells send messages."
