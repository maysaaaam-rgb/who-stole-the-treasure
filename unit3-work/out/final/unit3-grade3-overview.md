# Unit 3 · Grade 3 · Global Readings 2: "How Much Is It?" (pp. 40-55)

**Big question (book p.55):** Why do we need money?

**Topic:** Money: its history (barter to electronic money) and making good choices with money.

**Reading skill:** Identifying problems and solutions (p.47) · **Values:** Responsible decision-making · **Sound:** ar

## Readings
- **The History of Money** (Nonfiction (informational text with a timeline), pp. 42-45)
- **The $100 Bill** (Fiction (realistic story), pp. 48-53)

## Objectives (planning, not printed in the book)
- Pupils will be able to name 5 kinds of money from the reading (cowrie shells, metal knives and spades, coins, paper money, credit cards) and put them in time order.
- Pupils will be able to find a problem and a solution in a story and in an informational text.
- Pupils will be able to retell The $100 Bill in 5 simple sentences using the past tense (found, put, went, said, smiled).
- Pupils will be able to say what they would do with found money using 'I would ...' and 'I should ...'.
- Pupils will be able to ask and answer about prices: 'How much is it?' 'It's ... dollars.'
- Pupils will be able to read and spell words with the ar pattern (park, hard, card, car).

## Grammar in the printed text
- **Past simple, regular (-ed)**: people bartered for things they wanted (p.42) · Paige walked through the park (p.49)
- **Past simple, irregular**: he paid for it with grain (p.42) · Paige saw something in the leaves. (p.49)
- **Past negative: didn't + verb**: people didn't use money (p.42) · Paige didn't know what to do. (p.51)
- **could + verb (possibility / ability in the past)**: she could hear the girls laughing (p.49) · She could buy a cell phone now. (p.52)
- **should + verb (advice)**: You really should get one! (p.48) · I should save the money (p.52)
- **Comparatives: easier than**: easier to carry than goats or bags of grain (p.42) · easier to carry than metal coins (p.44)
- **There was / There is**: There was a beautiful black phone. (p.52) · There's no way to give it back (p.53)
- **Let's + verb (suggestion)**: Let's go out to a restaurant. (p.53) · OK. Let's go. (p.53)
- **would (imagining)**: What would you do? (p.54) · I would ... (p.54)
