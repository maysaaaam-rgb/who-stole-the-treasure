# Unit 3 Teacher Overview — Grade 3: "How Much Is It?"
**Textbook:** *Global Readings 2* (pp. 40–55) | **Target Learners:** CEFR A1 / Turkish L1 (Ages 8–9) | **Lesson Length:** 8 × 35 min on 1 Smartboard

---

## 1. Big Idea
> **Why do we need money, and how has the way we buy and pay for things changed over time?**  
Money is an agreed tool invented by humans to solve trade problems. Wise money choices balance saving for tomorrow, donating to help others, and prioritizing family love over material gadgets.

---

## 2. Core Objectives
- **Ask & Tell Prices:** Ask *"How much is it?"* and answer *"It is ___ dollars"* for classroom and everyday items.
- **Trace Money History:** Chronologically order the 6 money stages: barter (6000 BCE) $\to$ cowrie shells (1200 BCE) $\to$ metal tools (1000 BCE) $\to$ Lydia coins (600 BCE) $\to$ paper money (800 CE) $\to$ credit cards (1958).
- **Identify Problem & Solution:** Complete graphic organizers showing why paper money replaced heavy metal coins, and how Paige solved her dilemma.
- **Ethical Choices:** Use *could* and *should* to distinguish between selfish desires and family generosity.

---

## 3. Must-Know Words (with Turkish)
1. **bartered** (*takas etti*) — Traded goods or services without using money.
2. **metal** (*metal*) — Hard, shiny material (gold, silver, iron) used for coins.
3. **valuable** (*değerli*) — Worth a lot of money or very important.
4. **credit card** (*kredi kartı*) — Small plastic card used to buy now and pay later.
5. **plastic** (*plastik*) — Light, strong man-made material.
6. **belong to** (*birine ait olmak*) — To be owned by someone.
7. **return** (*geri vermek, iade etmek*) — To give something back to its owner.
8. **pocket** (*cep*) — Small cloth pouch sewn into clothes.
9. **restaurant** (*restoran, lokanta*) — Place where people pay to sit and eat meals.
10. **money is tight** (*para sıkışık, bütçe dar*) — Having very little extra money to spend.

---

## 4. Key Chunks
- *"How much is it? / It is ___ dollars."* (Asking and telling prices)
- *"money is tight"* (Explaining financial limitation without shame)
- *"belong to"* (Identifying ownership: *"Does this belong to you?"*)
- *"What should Paige do? / She could ___, but she should ___."* (Evaluating moral choices)
- *"I think ... / I agree / I disagree"* (Classroom discussion routines)

---

## 5. Grammar
1. **Price Questions:** \`How much is + singular\` (*"How much is the toy car?"*) vs \`How much are + plural\` (*"How much are the books?"*).
2. **Modals for Choices:** \`could\` (possibility / options) vs \`should\` (best moral action).
3. **Past Simple Narrative Verbs:** Regular \`-ed\` (*bartered, started, invented*) and high-frequency irregulars (*found, saw, felt, smiled*).

---

## 6. Phonics Focus: Spelling Pattern \`ar\` (/ɑːr/)
- **Target Words:** *car, park, hard, farm, card, smart*.
- **Minimal Pairs:** *cat / cart, pack / park, hat / heart*.
- **Turkish Learner Focus:** Lengthen the vowel to open back /ɑːr/ without over-rolling or trilling the *r*.

---

## 7. Readings in 3 Lines Each

### Reading 1: "The History of Money" (Nonfiction, pp. 42–45)
- Long ago, people had no coins and bartered items like grain and goats or traded rare cowrie shells.
- In Lydia around 600 BCE, people stamped the first metal coins, but heavy coins were difficult to carry on long trips.
- China solved this by inventing light paper money, leading to today's plastic credit cards and electronic payments.

### Reading 2: "The $100 Bill" (Realistic Fiction, pp. 48–53)
- Paige feels left out because her school friends have shiny new smartphones, but money is tight at home.
- Walking home through the park, Paige discovers a wet $100 bill hidden under autumn leaves and dreams of buying a phone.
- Remembering her hardworking father's exhaustion, Paige treats him to a warm pizza dinner, discovering family smiles bring greater joy than gadgets.

---

## 8. Values & SEL
- **Generosity & Family Gratitude:** Recognizing parents' hard work and choosing to bring joy to family rather than satisfying personal vanity.
- **Resisting Peer Envy:** Understanding that true self-worth is not determined by owning the newest expensive electronics.
- **Saving & Sharing:** Balancing personal savings (*Save jar*) with helping those in need (*Donate jar*).

---

## 9. How I Will Know They Learned It
- **Oral Observation:** In a 2-minute classroom shop role-play, pupils fluently ask *"How much is it?"* and state prices without prompting.
- **Timeline Sequencing:** Pupils drag and order the 6 money stages on the smartboard with 100% accuracy.
- **Comprehension & SEL Check:** Pupils explain why Paige took her dad to pizza using *"because money was tight"* and *"to make him smile"*.
- **Exit Ticket:** Pupils write one thing they would save for and one thing they would donate to.
