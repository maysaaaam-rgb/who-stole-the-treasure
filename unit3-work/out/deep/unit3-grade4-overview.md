# Unit 3 Teacher Overview — Grade 4: "It Keeps Changing"
**Textbook:** *Global Readings 3* (pp. 40–55) | **Target Learners:** CEFR A1–A1+ / Turkish L1 (Ages 9–10) | **Lesson Length:** 8 × 35 min on 1 Smartboard

---

## 1. Big Idea
> **How do changes in nature, climate, and technology affect humans and animals, and how can we protect our planet?**  
Rising global temperatures cause thin, melting Arctic sea ice that endangers polar bears and humans alike. Switching from polluting gasoline vehicles to clean renewable cars (electric, solar, biofuel) reduces global warming and protects shared habitats.

---

## 2. Core Objectives
- **Describe Environmental Changes:** Explain Arctic ice melting and habitat loss using cause-and-effect connectors (*because* and *so*).
- **Interpret Narrative Poetry:** Read and recite stanzas of "The Bear and I" with attention to rhythm, imagery, and emotional resolution.
- **Compare Vehicles:** Use comparative adjectives (*cleaner, quieter, more expensive*) to contrast gasoline cars with electric and solar vehicles.
- **Master Phonics (-ng):** Articulate the final consonant blend *-ng* (/ŋ/ sound: *sing, long, reading, bang, spring*) without adding a hard /g/ stop.
- **Formulate Eco-Solutions:** Use *instead of* (*"electric cars use batteries instead of gasoline"*) to advocate for green technology.

---

## 3. Must-Know Words (with Turkish)
1. **hole** (*delik*) — An opening cut through thick sea ice for fishing.
2. **twice** (*iki kez, iki defa*) — Two times.
3. **enemy** (*düşman*) — An opponent or rival; not a friend.
4. **thin** (*ince*) — Delicate, not thick; easily cracked under weight.
5. **trap** (*tuzak; kapana kıstırmak*) — A dangerous situation or place with no easy escape.
6. **melting** (*eriyen, erime*) — Changing from solid ice into water because of warmth.
7. **gasoline** (*benzin*) — Petroleum fuel burned in traditional car engines.
8. **energy** (*enerji*) — Power to do work or drive machinery.
9. **oil** (*petrol, ham petrol*) — Dark liquid pumped from underground to make fuel.
10. **noisy** (*gürültülü*) — Making loud, harsh engine sounds.
11. **electricity** (*elektrik*) — Clean energy stored in car batteries.
12. **expensive** (*pahalı*) — Costing a high amount of money; not cheap.

---

## 4. Key Chunks
- *"not enemies today"* (Recognizing shared vulnerability and mutual respect during a natural crisis)
- *"instead of"* (Choosing sustainable alternatives: *"clean cars use electricity instead of gasoline"*)
- *"made a trap for us both"* (Describing fragile melting ice that endangers both predator and human)
- *"protect our planet"* (Stating collective responsibility for global ecosystems)
- *"What happens when ...? / It happens because ..."* (Scientific inquiry and reasoning frames)

---

## 5. Grammar
1. **Comparative Adjectives:** Short root + \`-er\` (*cleaner, quieter, thinner*) vs \`more + long root\` (*more expensive*).
2. **Cause & Effect Clauses:** \`because\` (introduces the reason) vs \`so\` (introduces the result). Example: *"The ice cracked because it was warm"* / *"Electric cars make zero smoke, so our air stays fresh."*
3. **Substitution Connector:** \`instead of\` (*"Biofuel cars use corn instead of crude oil."*).

---

## 6. Phonics Focus: Final Consonant Blend \`-ng\` (/ŋ/)
- **Target Words:** *sing, long, reading, bang, spring, ring, wing, strong*.
- **Minimal Pairs:** *thin / thing, sin / sing, win / wing, ran / rang*.
- **Turkish Learner Focus:** Turkish speakers often produce an aggressive hard /g/ or /k/ release (*"sing-guh"* or *"sink"*). Teach students to hold the tongue back against the soft palate while vibrating vocal cords, releasing softly without a consonant burst.

## 7. Readings in 3 Lines Each

### Reading 1: "The Bear and I" (Narrative Poem, pp. 42–45)
- An Arctic fisherman sits quietly by an ice hole waiting for fish in the freezing wind.
- A massive polar bear approaches, but the unseasonably warm sun has thinned the ice, which cracks loudly under the bear's weight.
- Facing a shared trap, both hunters show mutual respect: the fisherman gives space, and the bear catches a fish and swims peacefully away.

### Reading 2: "Clean Cars" (Informational Science Text, pp. 48–53)
- Traditional cars burn gasoline from crude oil, producing loud engine noise and dirty exhaust smoke that warms the planet.
- Green engineers have created clean cars: biofuel vehicles running on plant sugars, electric cars running on quiet batteries, and solar cars powered by sunlight.
- Although clean cars are currently expensive to purchase, they eliminate toxic emissions and protect polar habitats from melting away.

---

## 8. Values & SEL
- **Wildlife Empathy & Coexistence:** Recognizing that human actions in cities directly impact the survival of remote animals like Arctic polar bears.
- **Environmental Stewardship:** Taking responsibility for reducing pollution through daily energy choices and green transportation.
- **Respect in Conflict:** Choosing non-violent de-escalation when encountering dangerous or stressful situations in nature.

---

## 9. How I Will Know They Learned It
- **Comparative Speech Check:** Pupils accurately state two differences between gasoline cars and electric cars using *-er* and *more*.
- **Poetry Performance:** Pupils recite a stanza of "The Bear and I" with appropriate emotional tone, pausing at line breaks.
- **Cause-and-Effect Organizer:** Pupils correctly connect the 4-step chain: *Gasoline exhaust $\to$ Global warming $\to$ Melting Arctic ice $\to$ Starving polar bears*.
- **Phonics Discrimination:** Pupils can accurately point to *thing* vs *thin* when spoken aloud by the teacher.
