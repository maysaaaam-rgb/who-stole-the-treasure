For the teacher: how to use this paper

Before: print two-sided on A4, one for each pupil. Tell them: "You can write, draw or tick. You do not have to answer everything. Only I will read it."

As a lesson (20 minutes): read the questions out loud with the class, show the sentence patterns on the board (I like … / I don't like … / My favourite … is … / At home I … / I want …), then pupils fill it in. It also gives writing practice at A1.

Questions 5 and 11 are private. Do not read question 11 out loud, do not show it to other pupils or parents, and do not promise exact seats. Use it with your own eyes to build partners: put each pupil near at least one friend they chose, and mix pairs so nobody is left alone.

Question 13 may hold worries. Read these first and follow your school's rules if a pupil needs help.

What to look for: Q1 and Q9 tell you which activities to plan; Q4 and Q3 give you topics for stories, games and warm-up chats; Q7, Q8 show who needs extra support; Q10 helps to make groups.

Keep the papers on paper. The platform does not store them, so no pupil's answers are saved online.
