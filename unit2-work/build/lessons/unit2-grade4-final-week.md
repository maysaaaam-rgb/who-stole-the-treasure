# Grade 4 Unit 2: Final Week Teaching Plan (7 × 40-Minute Sessions)
> **Course:** English / Primary ESL & CLIL  
> **Textbook:** *Global Readings 3*, Unit 2: "What Lives Here?" (pp. 24–39)  
> **Class & Level:** Grade 4 (Classes 4A & 4B), CEFR A1+ (Ages 9–10)  
> **First Language:** Turkish (Agglutinative, SOV sentence order, no grammatical gender, no definite article, vowel length contrasts)  
> **Classroom Environment:** ONE interactive smartboard at the front of the classroom; NO pupil tablets or individual devices. All learners participate simultaneously using mini whiteboards, dual-color voting cards (Green=True/Yes, Red=False/No), finger gestures (1/2/3/4), and team turns.

---

## Weekly Overview & Curriculum Alignment

- **Big Idea:** How are plants, animals, and humans connected in different biomes?
- **Reading 1 (Nonfiction):** *Two Different Forests* — Deciduous vs Coniferous Biomes (pp. 26–29)
- **Reading 2 (Folk Tale):** *A New Friend* — Go Ne, Wen Shu, and the Wounded Swallow (pp. 32–37)
- **Reading Skills:** 
  1. *Understanding Headings* (using bold section titles to locate information quickly)
  2. *Understanding the Author's Purpose* (To Inform, To Entertain, or To Persuade)
- **Phonics Target:** Vowel team `ai` (*rain, main, mail, pain, pail, tail, train, chain, claim, paid, sailed*)
- **Grammar & Language Focus:** 
  1. Purpose & adaptation: *"so (that) + subject + can [verb]"*
  2. Contrast: *"have/use [A] instead of [B]"*
  3. Narrative past simple: irregular story verbs (*built, fell, grew, paid, hit, flew*)
- **Assessment Target:** 50-mark Progress Check (38 marks written in Session 6; 12 marks speaking in Session 7).

## Session 1: Two Forests Comparison & Reading 1 Consolidation

### Aim
Pupils will differentiate Deciduous and Coniferous forests using physical adaptations, match wildlife to climate, and use bold headings to locate scientific facts.

### Materials & Tech Setup
- **Smartboard:** Interactive Biome Atlas: Deciduous Forest (colorful falling leaves, bear hibernating in den) vs Coniferous Forest (snow, pine cones, needles, white hare).
- **Pupil Materials:** Mini whiteboards, dry-wipe pens.
- **Target Vocabulary:** *deciduous, coniferous, hibernate, adapt, survive, seasons, leaves, needles, cones, camouflage*.

### Minute-by-Minute Procedure (40 Minutes)

| Time | Phase | Smartboard Display | Teacher & Pupil Actions (Whole-Class Engagement) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **00–05** | **Forest Rhythm Warm-Up** | Visual pulse graphic with chant: *"Green in summer, red in fall! Winter needles through it all!"* | Teacher leads rhythmic call-and-response with two hand gestures: spreading fingers (leaves) and pressing hands together (pine needle tip). |
| **05–15** | **Reading 1 Choral Navigation** | Textbook pages 26–28 with clickable bold headings: `Deciduous Forests` and `Coniferous Forests`. | Teacher models skimming by headings: *"Where do we look to find out about freezing -50°C weather?"* Pupils point to the right heading on the smartboard. Choral reading of 3 key sentences. |
| **15–25** | **Interactive Biome Sort** | 8 Animal & Tree Cards: *grizzly bear, snowshoe hare, pine cone, oak leaf, lynx, raccoon, broad leaves, evergreen needles*. | A pupil from Team Delta drags the *pine cone* into Coniferous. Class at desks holds up `D` (Deciduous) or `C` (Coniferous) on mini whiteboards (+5 XP). |
| **25–35** | **Language Anchor: Instead of** | Interactive sentence builder: `Coniferous trees have needles instead of broad leaves.` | Teacher explains *instead of* (yerine). Pupils complete board prompt: `The hare has white fur instead of ____ (brown fur).` |
| **35–40** | **Exit Ticket** | 3 quick-fire questions on board. | 1. Which trees lose their leaves? (`Deciduous`) 2. What do bears do in winter? (`Hibernate`) 3. What do coniferous trees have? (`Cones / needles`). |

### "If You Are Behind" Shortcut
If Reading 1 was only partly covered: Focus exclusively on the 2 big headings and the animal adaptation chart. Read only pages 26 and 28; skip the detailed climate paragraph.

### Exit Ticket Answers
1. Trees that lose leaves: `Deciduous`  
2. In winter, bears: `Hibernate`  
3. Coniferous trees have: `Cones / needles`

## Session 2: A New Friend (Part 1: The Wounded Swallow & Gold Seed)

### Aim
Pupils will sequence narrative events from the folk tale *A New Friend*, identify characters' actions, and analyze kind Go Ne vs jealous Wen Shu.

### Materials & Tech Setup
- **Smartboard:** Illustrated folk tale storyboard panels (Roof Nest, Baby Swallow, Gold Seed, Giant Fruit with Coins, Slingshot).
- **Pupil Materials:** A5 Character Response Cards: `GO NE` (Green) vs `WEN SHU` (Orange).
- **Target Vocabulary:** *swallow, nest, roof, seed, gold coins, rich, jealous, slingshot (catapult)*.

### Minute-by-Minute Procedure (40 Minutes)

| Time | Phase | Smartboard Display | Teacher & Pupil Actions (Whole-Class Engagement) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **00–05** | **Kind vs Greedy Warm-Up** | Visual dilemma: A shiny gold coin on the ground. Two path options: *Keep it secretly* vs *Find the owner*. | Pupils vote with finger 1 or 2. Teacher introduces Go Ne (kind boy) and Wen Shu (jealous neighbor). |
| **05–17** | **Story Phase 1: The Golden Gift** | Story excerpts (pp. 32–34) with audio narration. | Class follows along chorally. Teacher pauses: *"Why did the mother swallow give Go Ne a gold seed?"* Pupils answer: *"Because he helped the baby bird!"* |
| **17–27** | **Character Action Radar** | 6 action statements: *1. Fixed the bird's wing, 2. Used a slingshot, 3. Got gold coins, 4. Was jealous*. | Teacher displays statement. Pupils flash `GO NE` (Green card) or `WEN SHU` (Orange card). Board player taps matching character portrait (+5 XP). |
| **27–35** | **Irregular Past Verb Catch** | Falling verb bubbles: `build -> built`, `fall -> fell`, `grow -> grew`. | Pupils write the past simple verb on mini whiteboards: `The swallow fell.` / `The seed grew.` Teacher monitors spelling. |
| **35–40** | **Exit Ticket** | 3 story check questions. | 1. What bird made a nest? (`Swallow`) 2. What grew from the seed? (`Fruit / Gold coins`) 3. What did Wen Shu use? (`Slingshot / Catapult`). |

### "If You Are Behind" Shortcut
Summarize the first 2 pages of *A New Friend* orally using 4 picture cards. Jump straight to the Go Ne vs Wen Shu character sorting game.

### Exit Ticket Answers
1. Bird: `Swallow`  
2. Fruit was full of: `Gold coins`  
3. Wen Shu's tool: `Slingshot / Catapult`

## Session 3: Repentance, Resolution & Author's Purpose

### Aim
Pupils will complete the story resolution (vet visit, Wen Shu's gift, friendship), evaluate the Author's Purpose, and apply the *"so they can..."* grammar formula.

### Materials & Tech Setup
- **Smartboard:** Story Climax Scene: Veterinary clinic, gold coins payment, roof repair gift, and the 3-Door Author's Purpose Arcade (To Inform / To Entertain / To Persuade).
- **Pupil Materials:** Mini whiteboards.
- **Target Vocabulary:** *vet, heal, gift, repair, apologize, forgive, author's purpose, entertain, inform, moral*.

### Minute-by-Minute Procedure (40 Minutes)

| Time | Phase | Smartboard Display | Teacher & Pupil Actions (Whole-Class Engagement) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **00–05** | **Moral Compass Warm-Up** | Image of Wen Shu watching Go Ne spend gold coins at the vet. Question: *How does Wen Shu feel?* | Pupils show emotion faces (sad, ashamed, sorry). Teacher models word: *Guilty / Regretful*. |
| **05–15** | **Story Resolution Reading (pp. 35–37)** | Final paragraphs: Wen Shu leaves a special gift to repair Go Ne's broken roof. | Choral reading of resolution. Teacher asks: *"Did Wen Shu get gold coins?"* Pupils: *"No, he got a true friend!"* |
| **15–25** | **The Author's Purpose Arena** | 3 giant doors on smartboard: 🚪 1. TO INFORM (Facts), 🚪 2. TO ENTERTAIN (Stories), 🚪 3. TO PERSUADE (Opinions). | Class categorizes Unit 2 texts: *Two Different Forests* -> Door 1 (Inform); *A New Friend* -> Door 2 (Entertain). Pupils show 1, 2, or 3 fingers. |
| **25–35** | **Grammar Workshop: so (that) + can** | Formula tile bar: `[Subject] + [Verb] + SO THAT + [Subject] + CAN + [Verb]`. | Teacher models: *"The hare has white fur so it can hide."* Pupils write on whiteboards: *"Wen Shu left a gift so they can fix the roof."* |
| **35–40** | **Exit Ticket** | 3 Purpose & Grammar questions. | 1. Why did the author write *Two Different Forests*? (`To inform`) 2. Why write *A New Friend*? (`To entertain`) 3. Complete: `Animals adapt so they can ____.` (`survive`). |

### "If You Are Behind" Shortcut
Read the final page of the story directly. Focus the Author's Purpose on a simple two-way choice: *Facts (Inform)* vs *Story (Entertain)*.

### Exit Ticket Answers
1. *Two Different Forests* purpose: `To inform / teach`  
2. *A New Friend* purpose: `To entertain / tell a story`  
3. Complete: Animals adapt so they can `survive / hide`

## Session 4: Phonics Vowel Team ai & Narrative Verbs

### Aim
Pupils will decode, pronounce, and spell words containing the vowel team `ai` (/eɪ/), contrast with short /æ/, and use past irregular verbs in story sentences.

### Materials & Tech Setup
- **Smartboard:** The `ai` Rain Train (animated locomotive loading cargo labeled with `ai` words) + Irregular Verb Wheel.
- **Pupil Materials:** Mini whiteboards, Phonics Practice Slips (Worksheet 3 preview).
- **Target Phonics Words:** *rain, main, mail, pain, pail, tail, train, chain, claim, paid, sailed*.
- **Target Past Verbs:** *built, fell, grew, paid, hit, flew, gave*.

### Minute-by-Minute Procedure (40 Minutes)

| Time | Phase | Smartboard Display | Teacher & Pupil Actions (Whole-Class Engagement) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **00–07** | **Phonics Sound Studio: The 'ai' Long A** | Audio engine playing clean /eɪ/ vowel diphthong. Animation: Two vowels walking, 'A' talks, 'I' is quiet! | Teacher models mouth shape: open slightly then glide smile: `/eɪ/`. Turkish learners contrast with Turkish short /e/. Choral drill: *r-ai-n*, *t-ai-l*, *p-ai-d*. |
| **07–17** | **Minimal Pair Duel: ai vs short a** | Word pairs on screen: `rain / ran`, `pain / pan`, `tail / tell`, `pail / pal`. | Teacher says a word. Pupils write `1` (Option A) or `2` (Option B) on whiteboards. Board player taps the spoken word to hear audio feedback (+5 XP). |
| **17–28** | **The Rain Train Spelling Vault** | 6 incomplete sentences with missing `ai` words: `The dog wagged its t____.`, `The swallow felt p____.`, `Go Ne p____ the vet.` | Pupils write the missing word on whiteboards (`tail`, `pain`, `paid`). A pupil writes the letters into the train wagon on the board. |
| **28–36** | **Narrative Verb Assembly** | Spinning Verb Wheel: lands on `build`, `grow`, `fall`. | Team challenge: Change to past simple. Pupils write: `built`, `grew`, `fell`. Pupil touches smartboard to unlock the past tense card. |
| **36–40** | **Exit Ticket** | 3 Phonics & Verb items. | 1. Spell: `t-r-a-i-n` 2. Past of *grow*? (`grew`) 3. Choose: `Go Ne (pay/paid) the vet with gold.` (`paid`). |

### "If You Are Behind" Shortcut
Focus on the 4 high-frequency `ai` words (*rain, tail, train, paid*). Spend the remaining time on the 3 irregular verbs from the story (*built, fell, paid*).

### Exit Ticket Answers
1. Spelled word: `train`  
2. Past of grow: `grew`  
3. Correct verb: `paid`

## Session 5: Whole-Class Smartboard Review Arcade

### Aim
Pupils will synthesize all Unit 2 content (forest biomes, folk tale details, author's purpose, `ai` phonics, and grammar formulas) in a whole-class team tournament.

### Materials & Tech Setup
- **Smartboard:** Unit 2 Grade 4 Smartboard Game running in browser (Teams: Lynx, Bears, Wolves, Owls).
- **Pupil Materials:** Numbered answer cards (1, 2, 3, 4) for every child.
- **Rules:** Card answers raised simultaneously at desks BEFORE team representative touches the board.

### Minute-by-Minute Procedure (40 Minutes)

| Time | Phase | Smartboard Display | Teacher & Pupil Actions (Whole-Class Engagement) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **00–05** | **Tournament Briefing** | Game Title: *Forest Biome & Golden Swallow Arena*. 4 Team Scoreboard columns. | Teacher sets high energy: *"All eyes on the board! Quiet cards up first, then board player taps! 5 XP per correct answer!"* |
| **05–17** | **Round 1: Ecology & Folk Tale Radar** | 10 timed questions covering deciduous/coniferous facts and folk tale events. | Prompt: *"Why does the snowshoe hare have white fur? 1. To swim, 2. To camouflage in snow, 3. To fly, 4. To sleep."* Desks: All show Card 2. Board player taps '2'. Fanfare sound! |
| **17–28** | **Round 2: The 'ai' Spelling & Grammar Forge** | Word builder tiles and sentence gap-fillers (`so they can...`, `instead of...`). | Team Wolves arranges: `s - a - i - l - e - d`. Desks write on whiteboards. Friendly soft-fail chime if letters misalign. |
| **28–36** | **Round 3: Live Teleprompter (Speaking)** | 3-sentence speaking challenge: 1. Biome/Story Intro, 2. Formula 1 (*so they can*), 3. Formula 2 (*instead of*). | Team representatives come to microphone. Class chants background rhythm. Teleprompter text glows as student speaks (+20 Bonus XP). |
| **36–40** | **Medal Ceremony & Wrap-Up** | Podium display showing team rankings and gold/silver trophies. | Teacher awards star stickers. Quick whole-class review of the trickiest question from Round 1. |

### "If You Are Behind" Shortcut
Play the 15-minute quick tournament mode (10 rounds total). Dedicate the remaining 25 minutes to an open question-and-answer revision on the difference between Deciduous and Coniferous.

### Exit Ticket Answers
Tournament scores logged directly in teacher's gradebook.

## What Pupils Can Do at the End of Unit 2 (Can-Do Summary)

1. **Listening:** Identify forest animals, weather conditions, tree types, and folk tale story events from spoken descriptions.
2. **Reading Comprehension:** Use bold section headings to locate specific biological facts rapidly, and determine an author's purpose (to inform, entertain, or persuade).
3. **Phonics:** Pronounce, distinguish, and spell words with the vowel team `ai` (*rain, main, tail, train, chain, paid*), contrasting them with short vowel /æ/.
4. **Grammar & Sentence Building:** Construct sentences explaining animal adaptations using *so they can [verb]*, make comparisons using *instead of*, and use narrative past irregular verbs (*built, fell, grew, paid, hit*).
5. **Speaking:** Deliver 3 connected sentences comparing two forest biomes or narrating a folk tale moral using scaffolded sentence frames and accurate pronunciation.
