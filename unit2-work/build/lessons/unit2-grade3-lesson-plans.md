# Lesson 1: See, Think, Wonder: What Are We Thankful For?
- **Pages:** 24–25 (Unit Big Idea Opener)
- **Aim:** Pupils will name everyday things they are grateful for and use the sentence frame `I am thankful for [noun]` with visual prompts.
- **Duration:** 40 Minutes

### 1. 5-Minute Warm-Up (00:00–05:00)
- **The "Thank You Ball" Toss:** Teacher shows a soft ball. T says: *"Hello class! In English, when someone helps us, we say...?"* Class replies: *"Thank you!"*
- Teacher tosses the soft ball to Pupil 1: *"One thing that makes you happy: Mum, cat, or toy?"* P1: *"Cat!"* -> T: *"I am thankful for my cat!"* Pupil 1 tosses to Pupil 2. Rapid chain around the room (10 pupils).

### 2. Timed Lesson Steps (40 Minutes Total)
- **05:00–12:00 (7 min) | See-Think-Wonder Routine on Smartboard:**
  - Display page 24 illustration (two cupped hands holding miniature icons: house, ball, sunshine, game controller, bread).
  - T asks: *"Step 1: What do you SEE?"* Pupils call out nouns: house, soccer ball, sun, book.
  - T asks: *"Step 2: What do you THINK?"* T models: *"I think these things make children happy."*
  - T asks: *"Step 3: What do you WONDER?"* T prompts: *"Why do we say thank you?"*
- **12:00–22:00 (10 min) | Grammar Scaffolding & Choral Echo:**
  - Teacher writes on board: `I am thankful for __________.`
  - Turkish anchor: Explain that *"thankful for"* means *"-e minnettar, şükran duyan, teşekkür eden"*.
  - Whole-class echo drill with physical gestures: Hand over heart on *thankful*, open arms forward on *for*.
  - Board practice: Display 6 picture cards (family, food, warm coat, friends, school, toys). Pupils vote which one they want to thank.
- **22:00–32:00 (10 min) | Differentiated Partner Workbench (Mini-Whiteboards):**
  - Pupils work in pairs at desks using mini-whiteboards while smartboard displays visual word bank.
  - **Support Task:** Pupils copy `I am thankful for [word]` with a picture prompt and draw one item.
  - **Core Task:** Pupils write two sentences: `I am thankful for [family/food]. It is [good/warm].`
  - **Stretch Task:** Pupils add a *because* clause: `I am thankful for my school because I see my friends.`
- **32:00–37:00 (5 min) | Whole-Class Share & Smartboard Tap:**
  - Teacher uses smartboard random picker (🎲) to spotlight 4 pupils to read their whiteboard sentence to the class. Class responds: *"Thank you, [Name]!"*
- **37:00–40:00 (3 min) | Exit Ticket & Wrap-Up.**

### 3. Board Plan

+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+

|  I am thankful for [noun].                      - family       - food             |
|                                                 - friends      - sunshine         |
|  TURKISH ANCHOR:                                - home         - books            |
|  "I am thankful for..." = "... için teşekkür    - school       - toys             |
|   ederim / minnettarım."                                                          |
|                                                 GESTURE: Hand on heart -> arms out|
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 4. Classroom Language
- **Teacher Says:** *"Look at page 24. Point to the yellow sun. Put your hand on your heart: 'I am thankful for...'. Can you say it together?"*
- **Pupils Say:** *"I am thankful for my family." / "I am thankful for sunny days." / "Thank you, teacher!"*

### 5. Exit Ticket (3 Questions)
1. *Complete: I am thankful _____ my dog.* -> **Answer:** for
2. *True or False: 'Thankful' means feeling happy for what you have.* -> **Answer:** True
3. *Say one thing you are thankful for today.* -> **Answer:** (Pupil names any valid noun, e.g., *"My mother / My home"*).

### 6. Common Mistakes for Turkish Learners
- **Omission of 'for':** Turkish pupils say *"I am thankful my family"* because Turkish uses dative case without preposition (*aileme teşekkür ederim*). Fix: Drill the two-word chunk *THANKFUL FOR* as an inseparable unit.
- **Confusing 'Thank you' and 'Thankful':** Saying *"I am thank you for..."*. Fix: Remind pupils *thankful* is a feeling word (adjective).

### 7. Homework Option
- Draw 3 things in your bedroom you are thankful for in your notebook and label each: `I am thankful for my [bed/book/toy].` (No internet needed).

# Lesson 2: Reading 1: Chuseok, Korea's Harvest Festival
- **Pages:** 26–27
- **Aim:** Pupils will read about Chuseok, understand the concept of a harvest festival, and identify the month-long weaving contest.
- **Duration:** 40 Minutes

### 1. 5-Minute Warm-Up (00:00–05:00)
- **Autumn Harvest Mystery Box:** Teacher brings an apple and an ear of corn (or shows emoji on smartboard: 🍎🌽🌾).
- T asks: *"What season is it when farmers pick apples and corn?"* Class: *"Autumn!"* T: *"Yes! In autumn, we gather food. This is called the HARVEST!"* Class echoes: *"HAR-VEST!"*

### 2. Timed Lesson Steps
- **05:00–12:00 (7 min) | Vocabulary Ignition (Pages 26–27):**
  - Smartboard presents 4 target words with picture cards: `harvest`, `festival`, `weave`, `cloth`.
  - TPR Action:
    - *harvest:* Miming gathering wheat into arms.
    - *weave:* Crossing index fingers over and under like a loom.
    - *cloth:* Stroking shirt sleeve.
    - *contest:* Raising fists like athletes trying to win.
- **12:00–22:00 (10 min) | Interactive Shared Reading (Smartboard Text & Audio):**
  - Read lines 1–12 of *Chuseok: Korea's Harvest Festival*.
  - Teacher reads aloud once with dramatic tone; pupils follow with finger in book.
  - Choral read: Boys read Team 1 lines; Girls read Team 2 lines.
  - Comprehension stop: *"How long did the two teams weave cloth?"* -> *"One month!"* *"Who chose the winner under the full moon?"* -> *"The king!"*
- **22:00–32:00 (10 min) | Differentiated Comprehension Task:**
  - **Support Task:** Match pictures on worksheet to words: `weaving loom`, `full moon`, `king`, `cloth`.
  - **Core Task:** Answer 3 short text questions: 1. What country is Chuseok in? (Korea) 2. What do the teams make? (Cloth) 3. What does the king give? (A feast).
  - **Stretch Task:** Complete a summary sentence: `At Chuseok, people celebrate the autumn harvest and two teams weave cloth for a month.`
- **32:00–37:00 (5 min) | King's Feast Game (Whole-Class Smartboard):**
  - Display royal banquet table on smartboard. Teams tap correct story answers to place harvest fruits on the king's feast table.
- **37:00–40:00 (3 min) | Exit Ticket & Wrap-Up.**

### 4. Classroom Language
- **Teacher Says:** *"Look at page 27. Point to the woman weaving cloth. Weave, weave, cross the thread! Who is judging under the moon?"*
- **Pupils Say:** *"The king!" / "They weave red and blue cloth." / "Harvest time is in autumn."*

### 5. Exit Ticket (3 Questions)
1. *What do the teams weave in the contest?* -> **Answer:** Cloth
2. *How long does the contest last?* -> **Answer:** One month
3. *Who chooses the winner?* -> **Answer:** The king

### 6. Common Mistakes for Turkish Learners
- **Pronouncing 'weave' as 'veav':** Turkish 'v' is soft and labiodental. Fix: Round lips like blowing a candle for English /w/: *"W-W-Weave"*.
- **Confusing 'contest' with 'test':** Explaining that a *contest* is a competition between teams, while a *test* is a school exam.

### 7. Homework Option

# Lesson 3: Words in Context: Celebrating the Harvest
- **Pages:** 28–29
- **Aim:** Pupils will learn the vocabulary of cultural traditions (*songpyeon, ancestors, ceremony, dance*) and use `There are [plural]` to describe feast items.
- **Duration:** 40 Minutes

### 1. 5-Minute Warm-Up (00:00–05:00)
- **Flashcard Rhythm Clap:** Teacher claps 4 beats: *"Clap, clap, HAR-VEST! Clap, clap, WEAVE-CLOTH!"* Class repeats in rhythm. Accelerate the tempo for energy.

### 2. Timed Lesson Steps
- **05:00–12:00 (7 min) | Vocabulary Deep Dive (Pages 28–29):**
  - Introduce `songpyeon` (half-moon rice cakes), `ancestors` (grandparents of long ago), `ceremony` (special respectful event).
  - Turkish connection: Compare *ancestors* to Turkish *atalarımız* and *ceremony* to *bayram töreni*.
- **12:00–22:00 (10 min) | Close Reading: Ganggangsullae & Ancestor Respect:**
  - Read page 28 text together.
  - Video-still discussion on smartboard: Korean women holding hands in a giant circle under the full moon.
  - Ask: *"What are they doing?"* -> *"They are singing and dancing in a circle."*
- **22:00–32:00 (10 min) | Differentiated Practice:**
  - **Support Task:** Circle `There is` (1 cake) vs `There are` (5 cakes) with pictures.
  - **Core Task:** Fill in the blanks: `Families remember their __________. They eat delicious __________ rice cakes.`
  - **Stretch Task:** Write two sentences about the full moon dance using `under the full moon`: `Women dance in a circle under the full moon.`
- **32:00–37:00 (5 min) | Circle Dance Kinesthetic Simulation:**
  - Whole class stands by desks, holds hands, and takes 4 steps left, 4 steps right while saying: *"Full moon shining bright, Ganggangsullae through the night!"*
- **37:00–40:00 (3 min) | Exit Ticket & Wrap-Up.**

### 4. Classroom Language
- **Teacher Says:** *"Look at the plate of rice cakes. Are there one or many? There are many songpyeon!"*
- **Pupils Say:** *"There are delicious rice cakes." / "They dance in a big circle."*

### 5. Exit Ticket (3 Questions)
1. *What special rice cakes do families eat at Chuseok?* -> **Answer:** Songpyeon
2. *Who do families remember during the morning ceremony?* -> **Answer:** Ancestors
3. *Complete: There _____ a big full moon.* -> **Answer:** is

### 6. Common Mistakes for Turkish Learners
- **Pronouncing 'ancestors':** Turkish speakers struggle with the stress and three syllables. Chunk it: *"AN-ces-tors"*.
- **Singular/Plural agreement:** Turkish uses singular noun after numerals (*üç elma*), causing errors like *"There are three cake"*. Fix: Emphasize adding `-s`: *"three cakeS"*.

# Lesson 4: Comprehension & Traditions: Food, Dance, and Ceremony
- **Pages:** 28–29
- **Aim:** Pupils will synthesize the Chuseok traditions and answer comprehension questions comparing modern holidays to historical harvest feasts.
- **Duration:** 40 Minutes

### 1. 5-Minute Warm-Up (00:00–05:00)
- **Holiday Brainstorm:** T writes on board: `HOLIDAY / FESTIVAL`. T asks: *"In Turkey, what holidays do we celebrate with family and food?"* Pupils name: *Ramazan Bayramı, Kurban Bayramı, 23 Nisan*. T bridges: *"Yes! Just like in Korea, we visit family and eat special food!"*

### 2. Timed Lesson Steps
- **05:00–14:00 (9 min) | Chronological Sequencing Activity (Smartboard):**
  - Smartboard displays 4 scrambled story events:
    1. Two teams weave cloth for a month.
    2. The king judges the cloth and gives a feast.
    3. Families hold a ceremony for ancestors.
    4. Women and girls dance Ganggangsullae under the full moon.
  - Team representatives come to the board and drag cards into the correct 1-2-3-4 order. Desk pupils show number cards 1, 2, 3, 4.
- **14:00–24:00 (10 min) | Grammar Focus: Why & Because:**
  - Teach question-answer pairing:
    - `Why do families travel home?` -> `Because it is Chuseok.`
    - `Why do they have a feast?` -> `Because harvest time is here.`
  - Desk pair drill with A/B cards.
- **24:00–34:00 (10 min) | Differentiated Writing Task:**
  - **Support Task:** Trace and complete: `They eat songpyeon because it is delicious.`
  - **Core Task:** Answer in complete sentences: `Why do people celebrate Chuseok? They celebrate because they are thankful for the harvest.`
  - **Stretch Task:** Write 3 sentences comparing Chuseok to a Turkish festival (e.g. eating sweets, visiting elders).
- **34:00–37:00 (3 min) | Peer Check:**
  - Partners swap whiteboards and check for capital letters and full stops.
- **37:00–40:00 (3 min) | Exit Ticket & Wrap-Up.**

# Lesson 5: Reading Skill: Understanding the Setting
- **Pages:** 30–31
- **Aim:** Pupils will define SETTING as *WHERE* and *WHEN* a story takes place, analyzing how the environment influences what characters wear and do.
- **Duration:** 40 Minutes

### 1. 5-Minute Warm-Up (00:00–05:00)
- **Setting Detective:** Teacher displays two images on smartboard:
  - Picture A: Snowy mountain with children in heavy coats and skis.
  - Picture B: Sunny sandy beach with children in swimsuits and sunglasses.
  - T asks: *"Where are they in Picture A? What are they wearing? Why are they wearing heavy coats?"* Pupils deduce that the place and climate decide their clothes!

### 2. Timed Lesson Steps
- **05:00–12:00 (7 min) | Teaching the Skill (Pages 30–31):**
  - Define `SETTING`: The place (WHERE) and time (WHEN) of a story.
  - Board Anchor:
    - `WHERE:` Classroom / Forest / Korea / Space Station.
    - `WHEN:` Autumn / Night / Ancient times / In the future (Year 3000).
- **12:00–22:00 (10 min) | Setting Analysis Table:**
  - Pupils open page 30. Look at the three setting boxes:
    - Setting 1: Traditional Korea (Village, autumn, ancient times). Characters wear *hanbok*, weave cloth.
    - Setting 2: School playground (City, daytime). Characters wear uniforms, play soccer.
    - Setting 3: Mystery Farm (Indoor greenhouse dome). What do they wear?
- **22:00–32:00 (10 min) | Differentiated Setting Investigation:**
  - **Support Task:** Match 3 settings to their items: Mars -> Helmet; Chuseok -> Songpyeon; Beach -> Sun hat.
  - **Core Task:** Fill in the Setting Detective Chart:
    - Setting: Planet Mars. Where: Inside a dome farm. When: The year 3000. Clothing: Space suit and helmet.
  - **Stretch Task:** Write why characters on Mars cannot wear shorts and t-shirts: `They wear space suits because there is no air on Mars.`
- **32:00–37:00 (5 min) | Smartboard Setting Sort Game:**
  - Drag items (campfire, spacesuit, rice bowl, moon rover, loom) into two columns: *Ancient Korea* vs *Future Mars*.
- **37:00–40:00 (3 min) | Exit Ticket & Wrap-Up.**

### 3. Board Plan

+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  WHAT IS SETTING? (Olay Yeri ve Zamanı)           CLOTHING CLUES:                 |
|  1. WHERE = Nerede? (Place)                       - Cold snow -> Heavy coat       |
|  2. WHEN  = Ne zaman? (Time)                      - Chuseok   -> Hanbok dress     |
|                                                   - Space     -> Helmet & suit    |
|  THE BIG SECRET:                                                                  |

+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 4. Classroom Language
- **Teacher Says:** *"Look at the detective badge on page 30. Setting means Where and When. Where does the story happen?"*
- **Pupils Say:** *"It happens in Korea." / "It happens on Mars in the future."*

### 5. Exit Ticket (3 Questions)
1. *What two questions describe the setting?* -> **Answer:** WHERE and WHEN
2. *If the setting is the beach in summer, what do characters wear?* -> **Answer:** Swimsuits / Sunglasses / Shorts
3. *If the setting is Mars, what must you wear?* -> **Answer:** A space suit and helmet

### 6. Common Mistakes for Turkish Learners
- **Confusing 'Where' and 'When':** Turkish pupils frequently mix up *Where* (Nerede) and *When* (Ne zaman). Fix: *Where* has 'here' (place); *When* rhymes with 'ten' (clock time).

# Lesson 6: Reading 2: First Harvest on Mars

- **Aim:** Pupils will read the fiction story *First Harvest*, discover the strange crops (pink potatoes, purple corn), and track the clues leading to the surprise setting.

# Lesson 7: Skill Practice & Speaking: Space Suits and Secret Settings
- **Pages:** 36–38
- **Aim:** Pupils will reveal the Mars setting on pages 36–37, master the phonics of final `-st` (*fast, last, lost, best, test, harvest, first*), and speak using the teleprompter frame.
- **Duration:** 40 Minutes

### 1. 5-Minute Warm-Up (00:00–05:00)
- **Sound Stop Game:** Teacher says words ending in `/st/`. Pupils stomp their feet once sharply on the `/t/`:
  - *"fa-ST [stomp]!"*
  - *"la-ST [stomp]!"*
  - *"be-ST [stomp]!"*
  - If teacher says *"fa-t"*, pupils freeze! High-energy phonics drill.

### 2. Timed Lesson Steps
- **05:00–12:00 (7 min) | The Big Reveal (Pages 36–37):**
  - Read lines 25–34.
  - Ruth and August ask: *"Can we go outside and play first?"*
  - Mom's command: *"Check your space suits before you go. And make sure you have a lot of air!"*
  - Whole class gasps: *"THEY ARE ON MARS!"*
  - Discuss: Why do they need space suits? Because there is no oxygen in the Mars atmosphere!
- **12:00–22:00 (10 min) | Phonics Workshop: Final Consonant Blend `-st` (Page 38):**
  - Write on board: `fa-st`, `la-st`, `lo-st`, `be-st`, `te-st`, `fir-st`, `harve-st`.
  - Turkish learner focus: Turkish avoids consonant clusters at word ends, often adding *ı/i* (*fast-ı, test-i*).
  - Practice biting down on the `/t/`: *"Stop the /t/! No extra sound!"*
  - Minimal pair board check: `pass` vs `past`, `bus` vs `bust`, `bet` vs `best`.
- **22:00–32:00 (10 min) | Differentiated Speaking Teleprompter:**
  - Smartboard displays 3-sentence speaking teleprompter:
    - *Sentence 1 (Scaffold):* `Look at our greenhouse on Mars.`
    - *Sentence 2 (Formula 1):* `There is purple corn and there are pink potatoes.`
    - *Sentence 3 (Formula 2):* `We wear space suits so we can breathe outside.`
  - **Support Task:** Read Sentence 1 and 2 in pairs.
  - **Core Task:** Read all 3 sentences fluently with character voice (astronaut).
  - **Stretch Task:** Deliver the 3-sentence speech to the class without looking at the board!
- **32:00–37:00 (5 min) | The -st Sound Vault Arcade Game:**
  - Launch `unit2-grade3-game-3.json` on smartboard for 5 quick team safe-cracking rounds.
- **37:00–40:00 (3 min) | Exit Ticket & Wrap-Up.**

# Lesson 8: Think Together: Harvest Connections & Reading Journal
- **Pages:** 39–40
- **Aim:** Pupils will synthesize both readings (*Chuseok* and *First Harvest*), compare Earth and Mars harvest traditions, and complete their Unit 2 Reading Journal entry.
- **Duration:** 40 Minutes

### 1. 5-Minute Warm-Up (00:00–05:00)
- **Venn Diagram Quick Toss:** Smartboard shows two overlapping circles:
  - Circle A: *Chuseok (Korea / Earth)*
  - Circle B: *First Harvest (Mars)*
  - Overlap: *Both celebrate harvest with a feast and say thank you!*

### 2. Timed Lesson Steps
- **05:00–12:00 (7 min) | Synthesis Discussion (Pages 39–40):**
  - T asks: *"How are both stories the same?"*
  - Pupils identify:
    1. Both gather food from plants (crops/harvest).
    2. Both celebrate with a big meal (feast).
    3. Both families are thankful for what they have!
- **12:00–22:00 (10 min) | Unit 2 Language Review Relay:**
  - Divide class into 4 teams.
  - Smartboard shows 4 sentence stems:
    - Team 1: `I am thankful for...`
    - Team 2: `There is a...`
    - Team 3: `There are...`
    - Team 4: `The setting is...`
  - Pupils run up one by one to complete the sentences correctly.
- **22:00–32:00 (10 min) | Differentiated Reading Journal Entry:**
  - **Support Task:** Complete the cloze journal: `At Chuseok, they eat __________. On Mars, they eat __________. I am thankful for __________.'`
  - **Core Task:** Write 3 sentences in the Reading Journal comparing the stories and stating personal gratitude.
  - **Stretch Task:** Write a creative paragraph: `If I lived on Mars, I would grow pink strawberries. I would invite my friends to a space feast. I am thankful for my space suit.`
