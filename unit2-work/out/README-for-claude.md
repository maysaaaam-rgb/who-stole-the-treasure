## 1. Executive Summary & Core Content Breakdown

Both Grade 3 and Grade 4 textbooks feature two contrasting readings per unit (one informational nonfiction text and one narrative text), supported by dedicated phonics/spelling boxes, vocabulary in context, reading strategy pages, and collaborative synthesis tasks.

### Grade 3: Unit 2 "Thank You" (pp. 24–39)
- **Big Idea / Inquiry:** *"Why do we celebrate harvests?"* (Activating prior knowledge about thankfulness, sharing, and family).
- **Reading 1 (Nonfiction, pp. 26–29):** *Chuseok: Korea's Harvest Festival*. Covers ancient origins (month-long weaving contest, king's feast under full moon) and modern traditions (3-day holiday, travelling home, eating *songpyeon*, buying new clothes, *Ganggangsullae* circle dance, and *charye* ancestral memorial ceremony).
- **Reading 2 (Science Fiction, pp. 32–37):** *First Harvest*. Follows a family harvesting bizarre colorful crops (pink potatoes, purple corn, striped tomatoes, orange peas, purple carrots) in a pressurized indoor dome. Dad worries about scarce water. Ruth suggests a feast; Mom offers to bake an apple pie. The surprise sci-fi twist is revealed on p. 37 when children don space helmets marked **MARS 3000** and check their oxygen air supply before playing outside!
- **Target Skills:**
  - **Reading Skill:** *Understanding the Setting* (recognizing time, place, and how environment shapes feelings and actions).
  - **Phonics:** Final consonant blend `-st` (*fast, last, lost, best, test, harvest, first*).
  - **Grammar & Formulas:** *be thankful for [noun]*, *There is [singular] / There are [plural]*, *Can we [verb]?*.

### Grade 4: Unit 2 "What Lives Here?" (pp. 24–39)
- **Big Idea / Inquiry:** *"How are plants, animals, and humans connected?"* (Forest biomes, food webs, ecological adaptations, and moral relationships with nature).
- **Reading 1 (Nonfiction, pp. 26–29):** *Two Different Forests*. Deep comparative analysis of **Deciduous Forests** (North America, Europe, China; 4 distinct seasons; leaves change to red/orange/yellow before falling; bears, raccoons, coyotes, deer, squirrels; hibernation) and **Coniferous Forests** (colder northern biomes with temperatures down to -50°C; evergreen needles and cones; hares, lynx, wolves, owls; adaptations including thick white camouflage fur in winter).
- **Reading 2 (Folk Tale Adaptation, pp. 32–37):** *A New Friend*. A modern retelling of the classic Korean folk tale *Heungbu and Nolbu* (흥부와 놀부). Kind Go Ne and his father return a fallen baby swallow (*제비*) to its nest under their roof. The mother swallow rewards Go Ne with a magical gold seed that produces a huge fruit filled with gold coins. Jealous neighbor Wen Shu deliberately injures a baby swallow with a slingshot (*catapult*) to obtain a gold seed. Go Ne rushes the wounded bird to the vet on his bicycle. Overcome with guilt, Wen Shu watches Go Ne pay the vet with gold coins. Wen Shu repents and leaves a roof-repair gift for Go Ne, forging a true friendship.
- **Target Skills:**
  - **Reading Skill:** *Understanding Headings* (structural navigation) & *Understanding the Author's Purpose* (to inform/teach, entertain, or persuade).
  - **Phonics / Spelling:** Vowel team `ai` (*rain, main, mail, pain, pail, tail, train, chain, claim, paid, sailed*).
  - **Grammar & Formulas:** *so (that) + subject + can [verb]* (purpose & adaptation), *have/use [A] instead of [B]* (contrast), and *Past Simple irregular narrative verbs* (*built, fell, grew, paid, hit*).

## 2. Structural Mismatches & Deviations from Unit 1

1. **Grade 3 Shift in Phonics Progression:**
   - In Unit 1, phonics focused on initial `cl-` blends and the long vowel review. In Unit 2, the textbook explicitly targets the **final consonant blend `-st`** (*fast, last, lost, best, test*). Note: Grade 4 Literacy Review had previously touched on `-st`; Grade 3 is now formally mastering it in both spoken and written contexts.
2. **Grade 4 Folk Literature Integration:**
   - In Unit 1, Grade 4 read Lewis Carroll's *Alice in Wonderland* (Victorian British fantasy). In Unit 2, Reading 2 pivots to an indigenous East Asian folk tale (*Heungbu and Nolbu* archetype). This creates an immediate, empowering cultural connection for Korean learners.
3. **Reading Skill Contrast:**
   - Grade 3 moves from *Sequencing events* (Unit 1) to *Understanding the Setting* (Unit 2).
   - Grade 4 moves from *Skimming / scanning* (Unit 1) to *Author's Purpose and Headings* (Unit 2).

## 3. Potential Classroom Snags & Missing Media Assets

1. **Audio Dependencies on Page 38 (Both Grades):**
   - Both books have a *"Listen and say"* phonics box on page 38 (`-st` for G3, `ai` for G4) that originally relied on publisher CD/audio tracks.
   - **Recommendation for Claude:** Synthesize clean procedural TTS prompts or include teacher script call-and-response boxes so lessons operate 100% offline without missing audio.
2. **UK vs. US Lexical Distinction on Grade 4 Page 35:**
   - The textbook highlights that American English uses **slingshot** while British English uses **catapult** (with small flag icons).
   - **Recommendation:** Keep "slingshot" as primary in vocabulary banks to match the main reading text, but accept both in quiz/worksheet keys.
3. **Deciduous vs. Coniferous Conceptual Load for A1+:**
   - The botanical concepts of deciduous vs. coniferous can be dense. Use visual tactile metaphors: "Leaf-droppers" (oak, apple) vs. "Needle-evergreens with cones" (pine, spruce).
4. **Surprise Mars Setting Reveal (Grade 3):**
   - In 'First Harvest', the setting is kept ambiguous until page 37. Interactive games should not spoil the Mars reveal in earlier stages of the lesson arc!
